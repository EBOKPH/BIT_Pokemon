The noise has stopped. The dust is still settling. You count eleven people who are not moving and you understand, with a clarity that only comes after something like this, that they are not going to start.

You were a village guard. Boundary post. Two shifts. Nothing ever happened. And then a Legendary Pokémon came through your village the way a storm comes through a forest — not angry, not targeting you specifically — and eleven people decided to follow it into a ravine because a Legendary-class Fragment would buy three generations of protection.

Twelve went in. You are the one who came back out.

In the rubble, half-buried under a section of collapsed rock, is the chest. Someone dragged it in as an afterthought — just in case, they said. Inside it, where it has always been, is the old PIP device. Salvaged First Order tech, passed down through enough hands that no one alive knows where it originally came from.

"A fragment chip that has never been activated remains in stasis indefinitely. The signal does not degrade. The Pokémon does not age. It simply waits." — First Order Field Manual, recovered fragment, Year 12

The reader slot already has a chip in it. You did not put it there. You do not know who did. It is warm to the touch in a way that no crystal should be warm.

The factions will have picked up the seismic signature. You have six hours before the first scouts arrive. You are injured and alone and the Legendary is somewhere in the rubble behind you.

You strap the PIP to your wrist. The beam fires. The Pokémon forms — real mass, real power — and it opens its eyes and looks at you with that specific and terrifying question.

Are you worth it?
